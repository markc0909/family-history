The Opalin story / Before the journey

A remembered home
beside the Bug.

Schoolchildren, a market square and the routines of a Jewish household: the family tapes preserve a place through the texture of everyday life.

Place names

Opalin / Vyshnivka

The researched town is in Volyn, Ukraine. Ruda Opalin is a separate locality across the border.

Locating Opalin

Yahad–In Unum identifies its killing-site entry as Vyshnivka, formerly Opalin, in Volyn, Ukraine. This supports the location identified in Ethan’s earlier family history. Similar names in Poland, including Ruda Opalin, should not be treated as interchangeable.Vyshnivka, formerly Opalin — killing-site profileRiva Gilinsky — Ethan Cohen’s family history

Doba’s Polish passport gives Opalin as both birthplace and residence. Ethan’s early history places the family there in interwar Poland; the current location lies in Ukraine. The family’s documentary place name remains Opalin.Doba Milsztejn’s Polish passportPassport identity and residence pageRiva Gilinsky — Ethan Cohen’s family history

The house, river and market

In the group conversation, childhood unfolds around a wood-fired stove and heated wall, washing clothes by the river, shopping for fish and kosher meat, and sleeping near the kitchen stove. A monthly market and market-square shops recur in the recollections. These are oral descriptions, not a reconstructed town plan.Talking of Opalin - 101:03–04:10; 14:47–16:56; 27:11–27:41; 31:26–32:18

Doba baked challah, with local non-Jewish teachers remembered among her customers. Hela describes gardens and potatoes, a beth midrash and two shtiblach, and her widowed mother’s tobacco, rolling-paper and match sales. Her estimate of 250 Jewish families is a recollection, not a census.Talking of Opalin - 1Hela Lederer Tape 1 - 2Opalin – 1, 14:12–14:40; Hela – 2, 00:26–00:49, 02:47–04:47

The wider family economy

Minnie’s account of the maternal Appelbaum family includes a watermill, ferry, farmland and an inn in or near a village transcribed Wulka or Volka. Many places share those names. The village is not securely mapped, and the businesses should not all be assigned to one person.Minnie Spitalnik - 1Minnie Spitalnik - 2Minnie – 1, 07:12–09:51; Minnie – 2, 28:32–29:58, 36:34–40:23

Stories about the ferry and Chełm appear in the group recordings too, alongside humorous Chełm tales. Folklore is part of what the family shared; it is not treated here as a record of a particular historical event.Talking of Opalin - 107:45–12:35
