The Opalin story / The Alter connection

A cousin in Tel Aviv.
A link across the generations.

Mark’s memory of staying with Israel Alter in 1986 leads to a published memoir, a filmmaker’s interview and an older family relationship.

Identifying Israel

Mark remembers staying with Israel Alter in Tel Aviv in 1986 and identifies filmmaker Naftali Alter as his son. A 2020 interview identifies the filmmaker’s parents as Israel and Pnina; the Film Archive biography confirms Naftali’s professional identity and 1947 Tel Aviv birth.Mark Cohen’s family recollectionsNaftali Alter interviewed by Asaf NevoNaftali Alter — Israel Film Archive

In the Dorohusk memorial book, Yisrael Alter names his mother Chaya Dvora, describes arriving in Palestine in 1936 aged twenty and recounts a Warsaw policeman refusing to help an assaulted Jew. The distinctive episode, trade and origin strongly identify him with the filmmaker’s father. The son’s retelling is not a second independent eyewitness account.Yisrael Alter’s Dorohusk memoirNaftali Alter interviewed by Asaf Nevo

Two incidents, kept separate

Israel’s published Warsaw incident concerns a policeman’s refusal to assist a beaten Jew. Mark’s separate family account concerns a British soldier in Palestine responding with an antisemitic insult when Israel asked for a cigarette light. Mark says Israel thereafter refused to speak English and remembers that refusal during the 1986 stay. The British-soldier story has no independently verified date or document here.Yisrael Alter’s Dorohusk memoirNaftali Alter interviewed by Asaf NevoMark Cohen’s family recollections
