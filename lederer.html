The Opalin story / Lederer connections

Hela, three sisters
and a Warsaw baker.

The oral history gives relationships; a directory, burial patronymic and Pages of Testimony begin to put names beside them.

Hela’s parents and sisters

Hela says her father was Doba’s brother and died around 1920, leaving her mother with three girls. Hela’s burial entry supplies the patronymic Chaya Rivka daughter of Binyomin. Taken together, these make Binyomin Lederer a strong identification for Doba’s brother.Hela Lederer Tape 1 - 2Johannesburg Chevrah Kadisha burial searchHela – 2, 03:25–04:25

The Yosefa/Jospa testimony names husband Beniamin, parents Eliezer and Chaya, and Opalin residence. Its original lists three children. Freida and Chuma’s testimonies share parents Beniamin and Yosefa, and an aunt Berland as submitter. This closely fits Hela’s household, but none of these forms expressly names Hela as a sister.Jospa / Yosefa Lederer testimonyFreida Lederer testimonyChuma / Khuma, born Lederer

Freida is a strong candidate for Hela’s sister Frida. The other sister is transcribed Nechama in the recording, whereas the testimony says Chuma/Khuma; that identification remains possible rather than settled. Yosefa’s handwritten marital entry may read widow, unlike the index’s married; the reading remains provisional.Hela Lederer Tape 1 - 2Freida Lederer testimonyChuma / Khuma, born LedererJospa / Yosefa Lederer testimony

Mendel’s bakery

Hela recalls moving to Warsaw in 1924, aged twelve, to live with her father’s brother Mendel. She helped in the household and bakery and attended evening school. The group tapes also recall staying with a Lederer uncle who had a basement bakery. A 1928 directory lists Mendel Lederer, Ogrodowa 24, among Warsaw bakers.Hela Lederer Tape 1 - 2Talking of Opalin - 1Mendel Lederer’s Warsaw bakery, 1928Hela – 2, 06:26–08:30; Opalin – 1, 21:39–24:06

An aunt in Denver and the Sosna family

Hela describes her mother’s sister Emma in Denver, whose husband delivered coal. Emma sent about five dollars a month and clothing after Hela’s father died. Her surname is tentatively transcribed “Ray”. This is a maternal aunt, not automatically a Lederer. American census, marriage and death records are the next focused route.Hela Lederer Tape 1 - 204:50–06:20

Minnie describes Doba’s sisters and a Sosna/Reuter connection. A testimony for Pesl Sosne/Sosna, born Lederer, names husband Asher and daughter Ana Roiter. That is a useful collateral match, but the exact relationship to Doba remains unproved.Minnie Spitalnik - 1Minnie Spitalnik - 2Pesl Sosne / Sosna, born LedererMinnie – 1, 37:45–39:38; Minnie – 2, 30:45–35:31

Hela and Harry’s separate journey

Hela gives her birth as 1912 and Harry’s as 1904, both in Opalin. She recalls marrying in 1934, apparently 30 December, and daughter Phyllis’s birth on 9 October 1935. Harry left for South Africa in August or September 1936, then moved to Bulawayo in November 1937.Hela Lederer Tape 1 - 212:30–14:10; 22:14–23:06; 26:13–29:27

Hela and Phyllis followed in June 1938. She names Warwick Castle and gives 27 June; the exact event represented by that date awaits a manifest. Her later account describes Scott Street, flats called Glen Eagles and a move to Johannesburg around February 1978. These dates belong to Hela and Harry, not Moshe and Doba.Hela Lederer Tape 1 - 2Hela Lederer Tape 1 - 1Hela – 2, 37:02–42:00; Hela – 1, 03:30–04:50, 11:33–15:38
